Should you worry?

It depends entirely on what your signal contains:

    Temperature/NTC signal — NTCs are slow. Real thermal signals have almost no content above a few Hz. The filter is doing exactly its job: blocking 50/60 Hz mains pickup, switching noise from the buck converter upstream, and RF interference. A 10 kHz signal getting crushed here is a feature, not a bug. ✅

    If you ever expect to track fast transients (e.g. a pulsed heater, a fast-responding thermistor in moving fluid) — 2 kHz bandwidth may be tight. A 16 dB hit at 10 kHz means you'd see only ~16% of a fast-rising edge. You'd want to raise the corner frequency (lower R, lower C, or both).

    For the comparator downstream — the comparator (CMP1) is driven by the filtered output. The 81° phase lag at 10 kHz is irrelevant for a static threshold decision, but if the comparator is hunting (chattering) on a noisy signal, the filter is actively helping suppress that. ✅

Bottom line: no action needed for a thermal sensor chain. The 2 kHz corner is well-matched to NTC dynamics. The only reason to revisit it is if your application demands tracking thermal events faster than ~1 kHz.
