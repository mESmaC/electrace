What does the RC network between the sensor and the ADC do to a 10 kHz signal? Use the engine, then tell me whether I should worry.

Here's what the engine says.

The filter: R1 = 1 kΩ, C1 = 100 nF — a first‑order RC low‑pass with a −3 dB corner at ~1.99 kHz (1/(2π·1k·100n)).

What it does to your 10 kHz signal:

    At 10 kHz the engine measured −16.1 dB, i.e. the amplitude is knocked down to ≈16% of input (factor of ~6.3), with −81° of phase lag.
    For context, it's flat through ~300 Hz, already −1.5 dB at 1 kHz, and rolls off at the textbook −20 dB/decade beyond the corner.

Should you worry? It depends entirely on what 10 kHz is in your chain:

    If 10 kHz is signal you care about — yes, worry. This filter is sitting right at your sensor→ADC path and it's gutting anything above ~2 kHz. A temperature/NTC chain almost never has real signal up there, so this is usually fine — but if you intended this node to pass fast transients, the corner is far too low.

    If 10 kHz is noise you're trying to kill (mains harmonics, switching ripple from REG1, EMI) — this is doing its job, and −16 dB is decent attenuation. Note your buck (REG1) switches much higher than 10 kHz, so its ripple gets attenuated even harder (≈−36 dB at 100 kHz per the sweep).

The thing I'd actually flag: the downstream block is a comparator (CMP1), not a true ADC. For a comparator you mostly care about clean DC/slow level, so the 2 kHz corner is reasonable anti‑alias/de‑glitch behavior. But the −81° phase lag at 10 kHz means significant delay near the corner — if your threshold crossings need to be time‑accurate, that lag matters.

Net: for a slow thermal sensing chain into a comparator, this RC is appropriate and you don't need to worry. If you actually need bandwidth past ~1–2 kHz at this node, raise the corner — e.g. drop C1 to 10 nF (corner ≈ 20 kHz) or R1 to 100 Ω (corner ≈ 16 kHz). Want me to bump the corner, or leave it?
