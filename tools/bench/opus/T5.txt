A couple of things worth your call, since both new flags point at genuine design gaps:

    The 12 V → 5.5 V conflict is real. Either add a regulator between J1 and the comparator (e.g. 12 V → 5 V or 3.3 V), or the comparator will see 12 V on VCC and let the magic smoke out. Want me to drop in a regulator block?

    RS-485 on a comparator looks off. RS-485 is a differential A/B bus and needs a transceiver (e.g. MAX3485/SP3485) — declaring the class on the comparator's single-ended in/out pair will keep flagging missing termination. If your termination resistor lives off-board at the cable end, I can mark it terminated_externally to clear the warning; otherwise I can add a 120 Ω terminator. But if you actually want a real RS-485 interface, the right move is a transceiver block. How do you want to handle it?
