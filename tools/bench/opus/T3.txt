Here's exactly what's wrong.
The problem: your +5 V rail is over budget

It's not just a warning — it's an error. Your +5 V rail (vout#2) needs 3.6 A but its source — the buck converter REG1 — can only provide 3 A. That's 120 % of capacity, 0.6 A over.

The math is simple — two MCU loads hang off one regulator:

    vout#2 (5 V): drivers = REG1.vout, loads = MCU1.vdd + MCU2.vdd
    Each MCU+Load block draws 1.8 A, so 1.8 + 1.8 = 3.6 A
    REG1 (buck converter) is rated 3 A max → short by 0.6 A

And it cascades to the input side: that 3.6 A at 5 V (~18 W) reflected back through the buck makes vout (the 18 V input rail) pull 2.35 A vs PWR1's 2 A limit — so your power-entry is over too. Fix the 5 V rail and the input overdraw shrinks with it.
The cheapest fix

Reduce the load, not the supply. Before adding silicon, the genuinely free fix is to check whether each MCU+Load block's 1.8 A is real or padded — these generic library loads often carry a worst-case number far above actual MCU draw. If the true draw is ≤1.5 A each (3.0 A total), you're inside budget with a one-field edit and zero added cost.

If the 3.6 A is real, then in rough order of cost:

    Bigger buck (cheapest real fix) — swap REG1 for a single regulator rated ≥4 A. One part, one line item, keeps the rail topology identical. This is almost always the lowest-cost path.
    Split the load — give each MCU its own 2 A buck. Two cheaper regulators instead of one big one; usually more expensive and more board area, but it isolates the rails.

My recommendation: confirm the per-MCU current first (it may be free), and if it's genuinely 1.8 A each, drop in a ≥4 A buck for REG1.
